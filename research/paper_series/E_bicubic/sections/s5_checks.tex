\section{How the certificates were checked}\label{sec:checks}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{The upper certificate}

The radius-$12$ certificate is a file of $268$~MB (SHA-256 beginning \texttt{3e71e227}). Three programs accept it.
Two were written with the solver and evaluate the $536{,}805{,}378$ inequalities from formulas for the changes of $E$
and $T$, in $128$-bit integers. The third was written for this paper from the mathematical statement only, without
reading the other two. It does not use those formulas: it enumerates the short states of Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc}
directly, applies the four moves literally, recounts $E$ and $T$ on the whole stacks before and after each move, reads
the views off the stacks, and compares both sides after clearing denominators, in unsigned $128$-bit integers; a
bound computed at start-up shows that no intermediate value exceeds $2^{127}$. It finds no violation, finds four
violations when $R$ is lowered by $10^{-9}$, and locates the same largest quotient as the other two programs. It runs
in $5$ seconds on four threads.

A certificate that is wrong in a small way must be rejected. The third program rejects eight damaged copies: $R$
lowered by $10^{-9}$; the entry of the tightest state lowered by $0.1$ percent; the entry of the tightest state with
two full views of twelve symbols lowered by $0.1$ percent; an entry used by a successor of the tightest state raised
by $0.1$ percent; a zero entry; a truncated file; a trailing byte; a wrong header. The full-view case matters: an
earlier test on complete states could not reach it, because it needs $24$ open chords. At radius $7$ the same program
reproduces the result of an independent check on all states with at most $22$ open chords (largest quotient
$3.191740267742$ at $U$ empty, $L=000000$). The per-size inequality $z_n\le R^{2n}$ also holds for the $34$
published values, as it must.

\subsection{The lower certificate}

The radius-$10$ certificate is a file of $16$~MB listing the integers $X$. Two programs, written separately, accept
it. Checker~A builds the flow from Proposition~\ref{prop:rev} in exact integers, checks positivity and that both drift
sums vanish exactly, and encloses $h$ in $160$-bit interval arithmetic. Checker~B does not use
Proposition~\ref{prop:rev}: it rebuilds the flow with its own reader and checks every balance equation (S) directly,
$8{,}388{,}608$ of them, in exact integers; then it checks (D) and (P) and bounds $h$ from below with correctly
rounded decimal logarithms, adding or subtracting one unit in the last place by hand and rounding every sum toward the
safe side. Both give $e^{2h}\ge10.086918840819$. As a test of checker~B, three wrong constructions are rejected
because (S) fails: pushes without the reference factor $q$, one flow value changed by one unit, and a pop that reveals
one fresh symbol instead of two.

A third program was written in the independent reading of the lower bound, from the statement of Theorem~L and the
file format alone. It checks (P) and (D), checks every equation (S) by sending each window, move and revealed symbol
to its new extended window in exact integers, and bounds the logarithms with its own directed rounding. It confirms
$e^{2h}\ge10.086918840819$ at radius $10$, with slack about $6\cdot10^{-13}$, reproduces the radius-$4$ and radius-$8$
values, and rejects damaged flows and the target $10.086918840820$.

\subsection{Limits}

Both methods converge slowly in the radius. The upper gap to the estimate is about $0.49/r$, the lower gap about
$0.026$ at radius $10$; it shrank by $0.014$ from radius $6$ to $8$ and by $0.0076$ from $8$ to $10$. A radius-$12$
lower certificate would have
$33.5$ million windows and needs a compiled checker; we expect a gain of a few thousandths. The two bounds use the
same machine and the same notion of a window, but not the same information: the upper weight prices runs of equal
symbols explicitly, while the lower rule uses a reference law that only prefers alternation. A reference law of memory
two or three, or a lower rule that sees more of one stack than of the other, may close the gap faster. We have not
tried.
